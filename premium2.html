I.

A Note on the Practice

PROTELLIGENCE exists for the moment a number is challenged. When a total-loss settlement is disputed, when a repaired vehicle returns to market carrying a quiet penalty, when an estate or a dissolution requires a figure no party can call partisan — the question is never merely what a vehicle is worth, but whether the answer can be defended once someone is paid to disagree with it.1

The practice is deliberately narrow. We undertake five classes of engagement: total-loss valuation disputes; diminished-value claims; expert reports and testimony; valuation for estate, bankruptcy, and divorce matters; and the review of insurance claim files, including CCC and Mitchell valuation reports. Nothing else. A narrow practice is what permits depth, and depth is what survives cross-examination.

Every engagement begins with a conflict check and proceeds under a written scope. The methodology is transparent and reproducible: comparables are disclosed, adjustments are stated, and sources are cited, so that a qualified reviewer working from the same record would arrive at the same conclusion.2 Where matters proceed to deposition or trial, the opinion offered from the stand is the opinion set out on the page — neither softened nor sharpened for the occasion.

The practice is national, and it is singular in the older sense of the word: one analyst, one standard, one point of accountability from intake through testimony. Clients do not inherit a rotating bench of junior staff. They retain the person whose name appears on the report, and that person answers for it.

I·a

Areas of Engagement

Article the First

Total-Loss Valuation Disputes

Where an insurer's settlement figure and a vehicle's actual cash value part ways, we prepare an independent valuation from documented market evidence — each comparable identified, each adjustment explained, so that the resulting figure invites examination rather than fearing it.

Article the Second

Diminished-Value Claims

A vehicle competently repaired is not a vehicle restored to its former market standing. We measure the difference — pre-loss value against post-repair value — from observable market behavior, and we say plainly when the difference is small as well as when it is large.

Formula-driven shortcuts are declined on principle; the market, properly documented, is the only authority consulted.

Article the Third

Expert Reports & Testimony

Written opinions prepared to the standards of expert disclosure, with the founder available for deposition and trial. Reports are structured so that counsel can walk a trier of fact from record to reasoning to conclusion without a gap in the chain.

Article the Fourth

Estate, Bankruptcy & Divorce Valuation

Courts, trustees, and opposing spouses share one requirement: a number without an agenda. We provide dated, documented valuations of vehicles and collections suitable for filings, negotiations, and the equitable division of property.

Article the Fifth

Insurance Claim & CCC / Mitchell Review

A carrier's valuation report is a document like any other: it can be audited. We examine comparable selection, condition ratings, and adjustments against the market record, and set out our findings in terms a claims file — or a courtroom — can use.

Reviews are conducted with the same rigor whether retained by the policyholder's counsel or the carrier itself.

II.

Order of Proceedings

Conflict Check

Before substance is discussed, parties, counsel, and carriers are screened. If independence cannot be certified, the engagement is declined — courteously, and at once.

Records & Data Intake

Valuation reports, repair documentation, photographs, and title history are gathered into a single organized file; what is missing is identified, and the means of obtaining it agreed with counsel.

Valuation Analysis

Market evidence is assembled and weighed under a methodology that is written down, disclosed, and reproducible — the same discipline in every matter, for every client.

Defensible Report

Findings are rendered in a report that states its data, its reasoning, and its limitations, in prose a judge need not translate.

Testimony Support

Should the matter proceed, the analysis is defended at deposition or trial, in complete consistency with the written record.

Principles, Briefly Stated

Independent & Conflict-Checked

No standing arrangements with carriers, repairers, or counsel. Each matter is screened before engagement, and declined where independence cannot be assured.

Transparent & Reproducible

Method, data, and adjustment are disclosed in full. The reader is given everything required to retrace the analysis — and is invited to do so.

One Point of Accountability

The analyst retained is the analyst who reports and, if need be, testifies. Responsibility is never diffused across a firm's letterhead.

VI.

Questions Presented

Q.1Whether the practice serves policyholders, or carriers, or both.+
Both, though never within the same matter. The practice holds no standing allegiance; each engagement is preceded by a conflict check, and the methodology applied does not vary with the identity of the retaining party. That, in substance, is what independence means.
Q.2What materials counsel should assemble before requesting review.+
The carrier's valuation report (CCC, Mitchell, or comparable), repair estimates and invoices, photographs, any police or incident report, and the vehicle's title and service history. An incomplete file is no impediment; the intake process is designed to identify and cure omissions.
Q.3Whether testimony is available at deposition and trial.+
It is. Reports are drafted in anticipation of examination, and the testimony given is in all respects consistent with the written opinion. Counsel will find no daylight between the report and the stand.
Q.4Whether the practice accepts matters in every jurisdiction.+
The practice is national. Market evidence is documented from verifiable sources without regard to venue, and records intake is conducted remotely, so geography seldom bears on either scope or schedule.
Q.5How fees are determined.+
Fees are quoted in writing once the scope of the engagement is defined at the initial case review, and are never contingent upon the conclusion reached. A valuation whose price depends on its answer is not a valuation counsel can use.
