TC 00:09 · Q & A

Audience questions.

Do you take plaintiff and defense work?

Yes — both, never on the same matter. The methodology does not change with the caption. That neutrality is exactly what makes the analysis useful to whichever side retains it.

What happens in a CCC or Mitchell review?

The carrier's report is examined line by line: how comparables were chosen, how condition was rated, and how each adjustment was calculated. The findings state plainly where the report holds and where it fails.

Will you testify?

Yes. Reports are written from the outset as testimony-ready documents, and deposition or trial appearance is available whenever the matter calls for it.

What do you need to begin?

Party names first, for the conflict check. Then the records: valuation reports, repair documentation, photographs, and title history. Intake is inventoried so counsel knows exactly what the file contains.

Where do you work?

Nationwide. Records-based valuation travels without losing rigor, and testimony logistics are arranged matter by matter.
