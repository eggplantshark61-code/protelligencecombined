Exhibit A The Dispute

Two numbers.
Only one can hold.

Every valuation dispute begins the same way. A carrier produces a figure. An owner, an attorney, an estate, or a court holds another. Between them sits a gap that settlement talks cannot close — because neither side can yet show its number deserves to survive scrutiny.

Software outputs arrive with authority they have not earned. Comparable vehicles are chosen without explanation. Condition adjustments appear without support. The record, as it stands, decides nothing.

PROTELLIGENCE exists for that gap. An independent analysis, built from the records, documented to be reproduced — a number prepared for the room where it will be challenged.

A-1

The carrier’s figure

Produced quickly, at scale, by a party with a position in the outcome.

A-2

The software’s assumptions

CCC and Mitchell outputs rest on comparables and adjustments that can — and should — be examined line by line.

A-3

The missing record

Options, condition, history, mileage context. What was never gathered can never support a number.

Exhibit C Method

Five steps.
No shortcuts on the record.

The sequence is deliberate. Independence is confirmed before a single document is read; the number is committed to paper before anyone asks what it should be.

Step 01

Conflict Check

Before any engagement: parties, counsel, and carriers are screened. If independence cannot be preserved, the matter is declined. This is where every file begins — and some end.

Step 02

Records & Data Intake

Title and build data, options, mileage, condition documentation, repair records, prior valuation reports. The analysis is only as strong as the record beneath it, so the record comes first.

Step 03

Valuation Analysis

Market data gathered and weighed against the subject vehicle — comparables selected for stated reasons, adjustments applied by stated logic. Every judgment is written down so it can be retraced.

Step 04

Defensible Report

Findings delivered in a document built for opposing counsel as much as for the client: methodology disclosed, sources cited, conclusions separated from assumptions.

Step 05

Testimony Support

Where the matter proceeds, the opinion travels with it — deposition and trial testimony, and support for counsel preparing to examine the other side’s number.

Exhibit H Questions of Record

Asked before.
Answered plainly.

H-1How are fees handled?+

Fee structure is discussed after the conflict check, once the scope of the matter is understood. Engagements are scoped in writing before work begins, and no figure on this site — or in this practice — is quoted before independence is confirmed.

H-2Do you work for plaintiffs or for the defense?+

Both, though never on the same question. The practice is retained by plaintiff and defense counsel, by carriers and by claimants. The analysis is the same regardless of who retains it — which is precisely why each side can rely on it.

H-3How quickly can a report be delivered?+

Timelines depend on the state of the record — a complete file moves faster than one still being assembled. A realistic delivery date is set at intake, in writing, and kept. Where court deadlines apply, say so at the first conversation.

H-4Will you testify, and where?+

Yes. Reports are written from the outset to withstand deposition and trial, and testimony support is part of the engagement where the matter requires it. The practice serves counsel nationwide.

H-5What should we send first?+

Only what the conflict check requires: the parties, counsel, and carriers involved, and a short description of the dispute. Once independence is confirmed, you will receive a specific records list for the matter — send nothing substantive before then.
