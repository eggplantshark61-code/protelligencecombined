Chapter 04 — Methodology

One sequence. Every matter. No shortcuts.

The value of an expert opinion is decided months later, in a conference room, under questioning. So the file is built for that room from day one — five stages, in order, documented.

Stage 01

Conflict check

Before any facts are shared, we screen the parties, counsel, carriers, and related entities against prior and active engagements. If independence cannot be certified, the matter is declined — quickly, and in writing.

Output: written conflict clearance — or a prompt, documented declination.
Stage 02

Records & data intake

Title history, repair documentation, photographs, prior appraisals, platform valuation reports, market listings — everything is logged, dated, and indexed. What we relied on is never a mystery, to us or to opposing counsel.

Output: an indexed evidence file with a documented chain of sources.
Stage 03

Valuation analysis

Comparable selection, condition and history adjustments, and market context — applied the same way every time, so the result can be reproduced by anyone following the same steps with the same data. That is the test we hold ourselves to.

Output: a reproducible analysis with every adjustment stated and sourced.
Stage 04

Defensible report

The written opinion states its conclusion, then shows its work: methodology, data, assumptions, and limitations, in that order. It is drafted on the assumption that the most skeptical reader it will ever have is the one it was built for.

Output: a signed written report, structured for disclosure and cross-examination.
Stage 05

Testimony support

Where the engagement includes it: deposition preparation, direct and cross support, and clear, plain-language explanation of the analysis to the trier of fact. The opinion on the stand is the opinion in the report — nothing new, nothing softened.

Output: consistent, prepared testimony anchored to the written file.
Chapter 05 — Standards

Why counsel comes back.

Three commitments, kept on every engagement. They are not marketing positions; they are the operating rules of the practice.

I

Independent & conflict-checked

No contingency fees, no volume relationships that compromise the answer, and a formal conflict screen before any engagement opens. If we cannot be independent on a matter, we do not take the matter.

II

Transparent & reproducible

Every figure traces to a source; every adjustment is stated and justified. A qualified reviewer following the same method with the same data should reach the same conclusion — that is the definition of the standard, and the test each report must pass.

III

One point of accountability

The person who runs the analysis writes the report, and the person who writes the report takes the stand. No hand-offs, no junior staff behind the signature. Counsel always knows exactly whose opinion is in the file — and who will defend it.

Common Questions

Asked before nearly every engagement.

If your question is not here, put it in the case review form — direct questions get direct answers.

Ask About Your Case
Q1How are fees structured?

Fees are discussed after the conflict check, once the scope of the engagement is defined — records review, full valuation, report, testimony, or a combination. We do not publish rate sheets because scope drives cost, and we do not work on contingency because independence drives value. You will have the fee structure in writing before any analysis begins.

Q2What is a typical turnaround?

It depends on the completeness of the records and the scope of the opinion. A platform-report review moves faster than a full litigation report with testimony support. Timelines are set at engagement and honored; if discovery deadlines are tight, say so in the initial case review and we will tell you plainly whether the schedule is achievable.

Q3Do you testify at deposition and trial?

Yes, where the engagement includes it. Testimony support — deposition and trial — is scoped and agreed at the outset, so counsel knows before disclosure what the expert file will sustain. The testimony always tracks the written report: same method, same data, same conclusion.

Q4What is your service area?

National. Records-based work — valuations, CCC/Mitchell reviews, report drafting — is handled remotely from indexed documentation. Where the scope calls for physical inspection or in-person testimony, travel is arranged as part of the engagement. State-specific procedural requirements are confirmed with counsel at intake.

Q5Can we see a sample report or CV first?

Yes. A redacted sample report and current curriculum vitae are available to counsel on request, following a standard conflict check. Use the case review form or call (000) 776-8355 and both documents will follow once the screen clears.
